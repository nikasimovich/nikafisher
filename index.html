I’m a writer, design strategist, and educator based in New York.
Currently, I’m a tenure-track Assistant Professor of Communication Design and I teach classes about publishing, interaction design, and internet culture.
I’m interested in how design shapes what people believe (...about politics, spirituality, culture, themselves). I’m also drawn to overlooked histories of technology, especially how people have used it to express themselves and find one another. Lately, I’ve been bringing writers, designers, and students together to make experimental publications and get their work in front of new audiences.
My writing appears in The New York Times, MIT Technology Review, WIRED, Fast Company, Are.na, and Dirt, covering topics ranging from the digitization of Serbian Cyrillic typefaces to trash metaphors in interface design, AI and spirituality, and the politics of selecting (and keeping) a name.
I also run Labud, a design studio specializing in strategy, branding, and web design for clients across fashion, art, and tech.
